Impact of Population Growth on Water Demand

sub-social-science-ai-1-1-2

Large and growing populations increase domestic and agricultural water demand, leading to over‑exploitation of water resources for irrigation and food production.

Agricultural Water Use and Irrigation

sub-social-science-ai-1-1-3

Irrigated agriculture is the largest consumer of water; expanding irrigated areas for dry‑season agriculture over‑exploits groundwater, threatening food security.

Industrial and Urban Water Consumption

sub-social-science-ai-1-1-4

Industrialization and urbanization increase water and energy demands; industries use large amounts of water and rely on hydroelectric power, further stressing freshwater resources.

Water Quality Issues and Pollution

sub-social-science-ai-1-1-5

Even where water is abundant, pollution from domestic, industrial, chemical, pesticide, and fertilizer waste can render it hazardous, highlighting the need for quality management.

Conservation and Management Strategies

sub-social-science-ai-1-1-6

The chapter emphasizes the necessity of conserving and managing water resources to safeguard health, food security, livelihoods, and ecosystems, and to prevent ecological crisis.

What are dams and how do they help us in conserving and managing water?

sub-social-science-tb-1-2-1

This subtopic discusses the role of dams in impounding rivers and rainwater for later irrigation, electricity generation, water supply, flood control, recreation, and fish breeding, illustrating their multi‑purpose nature.

Classification of Dams

sub-social-science-ai-1-2-2

Dams are classified according to structure, intended purpose, or height, and include features such as spillways or weirs that regulate water flow.

HYDRAULIC STRUCTURES IN ANCIENT INDIA

top-social-science-tb-1-3

Sringaverapura Water Harvesting System

sub-social-science-ai-1-3-1

In the first century B.C., Sringaverapura near Allahabad had a sophisticated water harvesting system that channeled flood water from the Ganga, demonstrating early hydraulic engineering.

Chandragupta Maurya Era Irrigation

sub-social-science-ai-1-3-2

During Chandragupta Maurya’s reign, dams, lakes, and irrigation systems were extensively built, evidencing large‑scale water management in ancient India.

Later Historical Water Structures

sub-social-science-ai-1-3-3

Examples include Bhopal Lake (11th century), the tank in Hauz Khas, Delhi (14th century), and irrigation works in Kalinga, Nagarjunakonda, Bennur, and Kolhapur, illustrating continued hydraulic development.
